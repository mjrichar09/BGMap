# Movie Map: Demo Video Plan

A three-minute motion-graphics video that introduces the movie map, explains how it was made, and pulls interesting stories out of the data. Clean, cinematic, and built around one recurring visual: 36,841 films as a cloud of light.

## Goals

1. **Show the map.** The cloud in 3D, regions lighting up, a film being found and its neighbors drawn.
2. **Explain the approach in under 40 seconds.** Plots → embeddings → map, without jargon.
3. **Tell data stories.** The most common kinds of film, what rates best, what earns most, how the mix has changed since the 1950s.
4. **Walk through famous films.** Where they land and who their neighbors are, including a few surprises.
5. **Be honest.** One beat on what the map can't do. It's more convincing than a perfect demo.

**Audience:** film fans and people curious about data or AI. No ML background assumed.

**Deliverables:**

| Cut | Format | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Master | 16:9, 1080p (4K if the renders allow) | ~3:00 | Main demo |
| Short | 9:16 | ~60 s | Social (scenes 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10, reframed) |
| Loops | 16:9 and 1:1 | 3 × 15 s | Silent teasers: cloud spin, remake pairs, time-lapse |

## Data rules for the video

- **Ratings come from TMDB, not IMDb.** IMDb's datasets can't be republished, and a video is publishing, so every rating and vote count on screen uses TMDB's, as the public site does. TMDB ratings track IMDb's closely: correlation 0.83 on these films, near-identical medians.
- **Revenue and budget come from TMDB.** They aren't inflation-adjusted. Revenue is known for 38% of all films but 99% of films with 5,000+ votes, so money stats are about well-known films. Say "box office, not adjusted for inflation" on screen.
- **"Popular" means one of two things.** Either "most films made" (count) or "most watched" (share of all TMDB votes), and the two give different answers. That difference is itself a beat (scene 4).
- **The dataset is films with 50+ TMDB votes,** released 1900–2026. Say it once in the credits.
- **The credits card repeats the page's credits:** the TMDB logo and notice, and Wikipedia under CC BY-SA. Posters are TMDB images, so keep them small and credited.

All numbers below come from the current map (`qwen3__text_plot__inst__nn30_md0.05`) and should be regenerated by the stats script (see Production) before final render.

## Visual language

Use the **Late Show** theme as the base, and the **Matinee** theme for "intermission" stat cards. That way the video looks like the product.

| Element | Late Show (base) | Matinee (stat cards) |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Near-black `#0b0807`, film grain, a slow projector beam | Cream paper `#efe6d2`, halftone dots |
| Accent | Amber `#ffb547` | Marquee red `#c8102e` |
| Titles | Limelight | Abril Fatface |
| Labels | Oswald, all caps | Oswald on dark letter boards |
| Numbers | Courier Prime, typed on like a screenplay | Same |

**Motion vocabulary.** Keep it small and repeat it:

- **Projector dust.** Points drift in from darkness and settle into the cloud. Used for the opening and the time-lapse.
- **Beam reveal.** A soft cone of light sweeps across a region, which lights up as its label fades in.
- **Neighbor lines.** Amber lines draw outward from a selected film, then the list of names types in.
- **Ticket stubs.** Stat cards slide up like tickets torn from a roll, with the perforated edge; numbers count up.
- **Film-strip wipe.** Scene transitions as a strip of frames sliding past, or sprocket holes sweeping across.
- **Marquee letters.** Region names snap in letter by letter on a letter board.

Pacing: one idea per shot, 3–6 seconds each. Every number gets a counter or a bar that grows, never a static table. Ease out (`power3.out` or similar), with no bounces. Hold the final frame of each stat long enough to read twice.

## Storyboard

Timings are targets for the master cut. The voiceover (VO) lines are drafts to tighten later.

### 1. Cold open (0:00–0:12)

- **Picture:** black. A projector clicks on. Dust in the beam resolves into 36,841 points, which settle into the cloud, now slowly turning in 3D.
- **On screen:** the "36,841" counter rolls up. Then the title, MOVIE MAP, in Limelight.
- **VO:** "Every dot is a film. 36,841 of them. And films that tell similar stories sit close together."

### 2. The hook: one film (0:12–0:24)

- **Picture:** the camera dives into the cloud and the search box types "The Godfather". The clapperboard marker lands and amber lines fan out to its neighbors.
- **On screen:** neighbors typed in, each with its similarity score:
  - The Godfather Part II (0.96)
  - Part III (0.93)
  - Gotti (0.88)
  - Mafia Inc. (0.88)
  - Prizzi's Honor (0.88)
- **VO:** "Nobody told it these are mob films. It read the plots and worked it out."

### 3. How it works (0:24–0:58)

Four quick beats, each one drawn as a simple diagram.

1. **Plots.** A Wikipedia plot summary scrolls past (*Alien*, say), with key phrases highlighting: "Nostromo", "distress signal", "creature".
   - On screen: "30,253 Wikipedia plots · median 515 words".
2. **Embeddings.** The text collapses into a strip of 1,024 glowing bars, a "fingerprint".
   - On screen: "A language model turns each plot into 1,024 numbers".
   - Keep the model name small: Qwen3-Embedding.
3. **Similarity.** Two fingerprints slide together and their match lights up: *Alien* × *Aliens* = 0.94. A third, *Toy Story*, barely lights.
4. **Map.** 1,024 dimensions fold down to 3: the bars become a point, and the point joins the cloud.
   - On screen: "UMAP squeezes 1,024 dimensions into 3".
   - Then the clusters: the cloud splits into 126 colored groups, which merge into 20 labeled regions.
- **VO:** "We gave a language model every plot. It turned each one into a fingerprint of 1,024 numbers. Similar stories get similar fingerprints. Then we squeezed that space down to something you can see."

### 4. The lay of the land (0:58–1:20)

- **Picture:** the beam sweeps the cloud region by region and the marquee labels snap in.
- **Stat card (Matinee), "Most films made" vs "Most watched":**

| Most films made | Share of films | Most watched | Share of all votes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romance / comedy / drama | 7,503 films (20%) | Sci-fi / action / adventure | 18.2% |
| Crime / thriller / action | 3,339 | Romance / comedy / drama | 13.8% |
| Horror / mystery / thriller | 3,060 | Crime / thriller / action | 10.4% |

- **Punchline:** sci-fi is only 7% of films but draws 18% of the attention.
- **Genre bar race (optional):** Drama 44% of films, Comedy 36%, Thriller 22%, Action 18%.
- **VO:** "Most films ever made are romances and comedies. But the films people actually watch? Spaceships."

### 5. Famous films and their neighbors (1:20–1:50)

A rapid tour, about 5 seconds per film. The camera flies between them, each film's poster card pops up, its neighbor lines draw, and three names type in.

| Film | Region › cluster | Neighbors to show | Why it's fun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Titanic (1997) | pirate, ship | Titanic (1953), A Night to Remember (1958), Britannic (2000) | Same sinking, told four times |
| Oppenheimer (2023) | war, history | Fat Man and Little Boy (1989), The Manhattan Project (1986), Fail Safe (1964) | Finds the 1980s versions of the same story |
| Spirited Away (2001) | anime | My Neighbor Totoro, A Letter to Momo, Suzume | A clean anime island |
| Barbie (2023) | magic, princess | UglyDolls, The Barbie Diaries, **The Stepford Wives** (both versions) | The plot really is about a too-perfect doll world |
| Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) | dystopia | Furiosa, Mad Max 2, Thunderdome, **Deathsport (1978)** | The franchise, plus a B-movie ancestor |
| Everything Everywhere All at Once (2022) | time travel, time loop | Reset, Redux Redux, **Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness** | Multiverse films, released the same year |

- **VO:** "Titanic's closest match is… Titanic. Three earlier ones. Oppenheimer finds the 1989 film about the same bomb. And Barbie's neighbor is The Stepford Wives, which, if you've seen both, is fair."

### 6. What gets the best ratings (1:50–2:10)

- **Picture:** the cloud recolors by TMDB rating, a viridis sweep. The high-rated islands glow.
- **Stat card, "Best-rated corners of the map"** (mean TMDB rating, clusters with 80+ films):
  - Music documentary and concerts: 7.4
  - Anime and Japanese drama: 7.2
  - Nature and science documentaries: 7.2
  - Samurai and jidaigeki: 7.1 (*Seven Samurai*, *Rashomon*)
- **Lowest:**
  - Shark movies: 5.5 (*Jaws*, *The Meg*)
  - Slashers: 5.7
  - Zombies: 5.7
- **Single best-rated films** (5,000+ votes): *The Shawshank Redemption* 8.7, *The Godfather* 8.7, *12 Angry Men* 8.6, *Schindler's List* 8.6, *Spirited Away* 8.5.
- **Insight beat:** "Can a plot predict a rating?" A film's rating correlates 0.52 with the average of its 15 plot-neighbors. That explains about a quarter of the variation, so the story matters, but how it's made matters more.
- **VO:** "Sharks are the worst-rated neighborhood on the map. Even Jaws can't save it."

### 7. Where the money is (2:10–2:30)

- **Picture:** points grow by box office, so the cloud turns into a skyline. Gold tickets rise out of the superhero and space regions.
- **Stat card, "Biggest earners by neighborhood"** (total box office, not inflation-adjusted):
  - Alien and space films: $62B (*Avatar*, *Star Wars*)
  - Superhero films: $60B, from just 167 films with known revenue
  - Princess and magic films: $49B (*Shrek*, *Frozen*)
- **Median gross:** superhero films at $179M per film, against $11M for romcoms.
- **Top grossers ticker:** *Avengers: Endgame* $2.94B, *Avatar* $2.92B, *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* $2.52B, *Avatar: The Way of Water* $2.33B, *Ne Zha 2* $2.27B, *Titanic* $2.26B.
- **Return on budget twist:** the best median return is from slashers and dance films, at 3.6× budget. Horror is cheap to make and reliably profitable.
- **VO:** "Superheroes earn the most per film. But the best return on a dollar? Slashers. Cheap to make, and someone always goes."

### 8. How movies changed (2:30–2:48)

- **Picture:** the time-lapse from the page. The slider runs 1950 → 2026; new releases flash amber and fade as each region fills in.
- **Data overlays** (region share of each decade's films):
  - **Westerns:** 10.7% of 1950s films → 1.5% in the 2010s. The western region visibly goes dark.
  - **Horror:** 3.6% of 1950s films → 9.3% in the 2020s. It keeps growing.
  - **Superhero cluster:** nearly 3× its 1970s–80s share by the 2010s.
  - **Also growing:** stand-up comedy, terminal-illness dramas and true-story films.
  - **Shrinking:** private detectives, 19th-century Russia, kung fu.
- **Side note:** median runtime has barely moved since the 1960s, about 97–100 minutes.
- **VO:** "The western rode off into the sunset. Horror moved in, and stayed."

### 9. Things it found on its own (2:48–3:05)

A quick-fire montage. Each item gets a 2-second split screen with two posters and a similarity score.

- **Remakes, found blind.** Nobody told the model these are remakes:
  - *The Lion King* 1994 ↔ 2019 (1.00)
  - *Psycho* 1960 ↔ 1998 (0.98)
  - *12 Angry Men* 1957 ↔ 1997 (0.97)
  - *West Side Story* 1961 ↔ 2021 (0.98)
- **Same story, different title.** *The Prince of Egypt* (1998) ↔ *Exodus: Gods and Kings* (2014), 0.97. *The Lord of the Rings* (1978 animated) ↔ *The Fellowship of the Ring* (2001), 0.98.
- **Same story, worse movie.** *Jaws* → *Jaws: The Revenge*: plot similarity 0.95, but the rating falls off a cliff. Show the similarity bar staying full while the rating bar drains.
- **Most original hits.** Popular films whose nearest neighbor is least similar: *Cloud Atlas*, *Poor Things*, *Midnight in Paris*, *Uncut Gems*, *The Founder*. "Nothing else on the map is quite like them."
- **Influence route.** The page's route tool: *The Matrix* (1999) → *The Matrix Reloaded* → *Final Flight of the Osiris* → *Inception* (2010). An amber path draws across the cloud.

### 10. Honest beat and close (3:05–3:20)

- **Limitation.** *Get Out*'s neighbors are body-horror B-movies (*Body Parts*, *The Evil Within*). The model reads what happens, not the satire or the tone. One line on screen: "It reads plots, not tone."
- **Interactive tease.** Quick cuts of the real page: a search, the people lens ("Akira Kurosawa, 30 films"), Movie night, theme switch Matinee ↔ Late Show.
- **End card:** the cloud spins, then the URL and the credits (TMDB logo and notice, Wikipedia CC BY-SA, "Plots: Wikipedia · Film data: TMDB").
- **VO:** "It's not perfect. It reads plots, not tone. But it's a new way to wander through a century of movies. Go get lost."

## Follow-up videos

The launch post is 45 seconds, 1:1 square, for X. These topics are each strong enough for their own short:

1. **"The movies nobody has made yet."** The site's *Unmade movies* tool (`src/gaps.py`).
   - **How the gaps are found:** every pair of the 126 clusters is blended in the model's full 1,024-number space, and we keep the blends that are emptiest compared with similar pairs.
   - **The pitches:** an AI writes a pitch for each gap, and the same model places it back in the space to check that it lands between both parents.
   - **Pitches that land:**
     - *Second Unit*: a film crew shooting a tsunami movie gets a real tsunami (filmmaking × disaster).
     - *Season Finale*: a reality-show castaway discovers the producers have gone home (TV satire × survival).
     - *Thin Air*: a Nepalese village's first Winter Olympics team, stranded on its training glacier (Olympic underdogs × mountain survival).
     - *The Heretic Princess*: a princess with healing magic is put on trial by her own church (faith × princess fantasy).
   - **Visual:** a pulsing "?" between the two parent clusters, lines drawn to each, then "closest thing already made" next to "if it were made, its nearest films".
2. **"The four ways movies differ."** The *Axes (PCA)* layout. The main directions of the embedding, each end named by its genres:
   - tender (romance, comedy) ↔ brutal (action, thriller)
   - adult deceit (*Gone Girl*, *Knives Out*) ↔ childhood wonder (*E.T.*, *Narnia*)
   - outer conflict (*Top Gun*) ↔ inner dread (*Hereditary*, *The Conjuring*)
   - solemn history ↔ silly fun

   PC1–3 explain 22% of the variation. Animate the cloud collapsing onto each axis in turn.
3. **"Original films are rated higher, but watched less."** Originality is how unlike its 15 nearest plot neighbors a film is, measured in the full space.
   - The most original fifth of films average 6.53 on TMDB with a median of 112 votes; the most crowded fifth average 6.26 with 231 votes.
   - Most original popular films: *Cloud Atlas*, *Poor Things*, *Midnight in Paris*, *Uncut Gems*.
   - The site's *Originality* color shows this live.
4. **"Follow the money."** The new box office, budget and return-on-budget colors. Superhero films earn the most per film; slashers return the most per dollar.
5. **The remakes episode.** Same-story pairs found blind (*The Lion King*, *Psycho*, *The Prince of Egypt* ↔ *Exodus*).

**A note for the video's explainer: map versus model.**
- **What's measured in the full 1,024-number embedding:** neighbors, routes, originality and gaps.
- **What's for display only:** the map's 2D and 3D positions, which come from UMAP. They keep only about 11% of true neighbors, and UMAP doesn't preserve density, so empty-looking patches on the map aren't real gaps.
- **What the PCA view is for:** explaining what the space's main directions mean. It's not for finding neighbors (its 2D view keeps 0% of them).

- **Hidden gems.** Highly rated films with few votes, shown as small gold sparks in quiet parts of the map.

## Production

### Approach

There are three kinds of shots, built separately and cut together:

1. **Map shots: scripted captures of the real page.**
   - Drive the page with Playwright, setting deck.gl's view state frame by frame (camera paths, rotations, fly-tos) and saving each frame as an image. This gives frame-perfect 60 fps footage with no screen-recording jitter.
   - Add a `?video` mode to the page that hides the panels, raises the point resolution, and takes camera keyframes from a JSON file.
2. **Data motion graphics.** Stat cards, bar races, counters, split screens and the "how it works" diagrams.
   - Build them as HTML compositions with GSAP timelines. HyperFrames (available in this environment) renders these deterministically to MP4.
   - Each scene reads its numbers from one JSON file, so a data refresh re-renders without hand edits.
3. **Titles and transitions.** The film-strip wipes, marquee letters and projector-beam reveals, built once as reusable components.

### Data prep

Add a script, `src/video_stats.py`, that writes `data/movies/video/stats.json` with every number in this plan:

- region and genre counts, and vote shares
- best and worst clusters by rating
- revenue totals, medians and return on budget per cluster
- region share by decade, and fastest-growing and shrinking clusters
- neighbors and similarities for each featured film
- remake pairs, the most original films, and sequel similarities
- the Matrix → Inception route

It should use TMDB's ratings, as the site does, so nothing from IMDb ends up in the video.

### Audio

- **Music:** a music bed, with projector clicks and film-reel whirs as accents.
- **VO:** recorded by you, or text-to-speech as a placeholder.
- **Captions:** burned in for the social cuts.

### Steps

1. Lock the storyboard and VO script; read it aloud and time it.
2. Write `video_stats.py` and check every number against the map.
3. Build the page's `?video` mode and camera keyframes, then render the map shots.
4. Build the design-system components: stat card, counter, bar, split screen, transitions.
5. Build scenes 3–9 as compositions fed by `stats.json`.
6. Rough cut with placeholder VO; check pacing (aim for no shot over 6 s without motion).
7. Final VO, music and sound mix; captions; 9:16 reframes.
8. Check the credits, and that no IMDb data appears anywhere.
